01 · RegulationEU supply-chain due diligence — the deadline is 2029, the demand arrives now

The legal basis is Directive (EU) 2024/1760, in force since 25 July 2024, revised by the Omnibus I package (deferral directive 2025/794; content directive 2026/470, published 26 February 2026 and in force 18 March 2026).

  • Who is covered — EU companies with more than 5,000 employees and worldwide net turnover above EUR 1.5bn; non-EU companies with EU net turnover above EUR 1.5bn.
  • Timetable — Member States transpose by 26 July 2028; obligations apply from 26 July 2029; Art. 16 reporting starts with financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2030.
  • Scope shrank, pressure did not — the covered population is about 70% smaller than the original proposal (1,000 employees / EUR 450m). EU-wide civil liability was removed, penalties capped at 3% of worldwide net turnover.
  • Why suppliers still feel it — information may be requested from suppliers below 5,000 employees only when it cannot be obtained another way. That standardises the format and scope large buyers use, so a supplier holding a ready impact card and due-diligence pack is the one that gets chosen.
규제 대상이 아니라는 말은 곧 요구가 없다는 뜻이 아닙니다. 대기업이 협력사에 요청하는 정보의 형식이 정형화되면서, 표준화된 임팩트 카드·실사 양식을 미리 갖춘 공급사가 선택됩니다.

02 · Data marketSupplier due diligence is already an industry

  • EcoVadis has rated 175,000 companies in 180+ countries and screens 3 million suppliers; 25,852 companies joined in 2025 alone.
  • About 1,400 purchasing organisations use the rating as a selection criterion, covering USD 2.5tn of spend. 84% of rated companies have 25–999 employees — the SME segment is already institutionalised.
  • Korea: assessments grew 56% year on year in 2025 (860+ cases).
  • Implication: align our impact card fields with the EcoVadis questionnaire (environment, labour, ethics, sustainable procurement) so a supplier answers once and owns a due-diligence record.

03 · Social procurementBuy Social: proof that purchase budgets move

Buy Social Corporate Challenge (UK, SEUK × government)

36 corporates, target of GBP 1bn to social enterprises. Cumulative spend 2016–2025: GBP 864.4m; in 2025 alone GBP 209.3m. 2,583 social enterprises have traded (1,349 in 2025, +19%). 20 suppliers hold contracts over GBP 1m a year; 21 trade with 10+ corporates. GBP 78m reinvested, 7,863 jobs created; 68% of participants report greater supply-chain diversity. Phase 2 (BSCC v2.0) begins in 2027.

Buy Social Canada

Certified Social Enterprise mark + directory + buyer network; certification fee, no transaction commission. 2024 survey: 182 certified enterprises, CAD 5.9bn revenue, 12,215 jobs (over half held by people facing barriers). 2026 survey: 347 organisations / 404 social enterprises. 19% of enterprises bid on tenders that award points for CSE certification.

04 · CertificationFairtrade in numbers (2024)

1,824 certified producer organisations and 1,871,272 farmers and workers (about 1.87 million, 90% farmers). Premiums paid to producers in 2024: EUR 201.6m (over EUR 1bn in five years). 1.5 million tonnes of certified volume sold; top premiums cocoa EUR 73.4m, coffee EUR 57.6m, banana EUR 36.4m. The number of certified organisations fell from 1,930 (2020) to 1,824 (2024) — the certification market is maturing and differentiation has moved to how the trade is done.

05 · Korea policyDomestic policy is pushing in the same direction

  • Social Economy Development Plan (30 June 2026, 20 ministries): 3 strategies, 15 tasks. Target: social economy from 0.8% to 10% of GDP by 2035 (employment 1.8% → 10%); leading companies from ~250 (2024) to 1,000 by 2030 and 2,000 by 2035. Average social-economy organisation today: KRW 588m revenue, 10.2 staff.
  • Certified social enterprises (Dec 2025): 3,766 enterprises, 71,950 jobs (46,385 held by vulnerable groups), KRW 7.285tn revenue; 33.9% rated 'excellent/outstanding' on the Social Value Index; KRW 283.9bn reinvested.
  • Export vouchers 2026 (MSS/KOSME): budget KRW 150.2bn (+KRW 22.6bn). Limits KRW 30m (domestic/beginner) to KRW 100m (small champion); government funding 50–70%; up to KRW 150m with the tariff-response package; international transport up to KRW 60m.

The shared limit of public programmes

Most fund activities and consultations. None provides a standing desk that quotes, samples, inspects, ships and settles after the contract. Cohorts are small (10, 15, 25 companies), so a company that is not selected waits a year. The platform's role is not to replace these programmes but to convert the contacts they create into recurring trade.

지원사업은 '활동비'와 '상담'을 지원합니다. 계약 이후의 견적·샘플·검품·선적·정산을 상시 대행하는 창구가 비어 있는 지점이 우리의 자리입니다.

07 · Korean casesFive exports, and what actually blocked them

Donggubat 동구밭 — employment story turned into an export spec

USD 140,000 to US · JP · CN · IN (2018) · KRW 12bn revenue (2021) · 90 staff, half with developmental disabilities

Eco-friendly soap made by people with developmental disabilities. Buyers included Amorepacific, LG H&H and CJ Olive Young; listed at E-mart. The purchase reason abroad was the product plus the employment story — but the channel depended on fairs, consultants and corporate partnerships.

Lesson — a social story opens the conversation; the product and volume have to stand on their own.

119REO 119레오 — specifications opened a B2B component market

KRW 715m revenue (2025) · ~KRW 200m donated cumulatively

Upcycled fire-suits into bags and wallets, 50% of operating profit donated to firefighters. KOTRA's consumer-goods export fair in 2019 produced the first Japanese order. Later, recycled aramid fibre became a lithium-battery fire-containment bag supplied to 9 Korean airlines.

Lesson — 'eco' is the entry ticket; the standard is the contract. Certification and specs move a craft brand into safety-critical B2B.

KOIRO (코이로) × 5 social enterprises — joint booth, joint export

EUR 4,400 exported · stock sold out · Milan, ARTIGIANO IN FIERA 2025

Five social enterprises shared one booth and one export agent. Individually, booth cost, interpretation and logistics would have sunk each of them.

코이로가 사회적기업 5개사와 2025년 밀라노 '아르티지아노 인 피에라(ARTIGIANO IN FIERA)'에 공동 부스로 참가해 약 4,400유로를 수출하고 준비 물량을 완판했습니다. 다섯 곳이 각자 나갔다면 부스비·통역·물류에서 먼저 무너졌을 규모입니다.
Lesson — the first export happens when someone carries the shared cost. The size stays small without a repeat mechanism.

Gyeongbuk buyer meeting — 74 consultations, 46 MOUs, USD 15,000 signed

2026-10 · 25 local companies · 15 overseas buyers · consultations worth USD 81,740

The gap between consultation value and signed contracts is the whole problem. MOUs are abundant; after-signature execution is not.

Lesson — the bottleneck is quotation, sampling, documents and logistics. This is exactly the platform's first product.

Marco Roho 마르코로호 — demand for export support is real

8 years without an overseas channel (2023 interview) · 60%+ of revenue from a single leaf ring

A social venture in Sangju making accessories with elderly knot artisans, donating 20% of the sale price. Good product and story, no route abroad.

Lesson — the barrier is procedure, not will. A guided first order beats another brochure.

08 · What it means for usTen design decisions this research settled

  • Buyers free, suppliers carry cost — the common structure of Faire, Ankorstore and GoCoop.
  • Commission at 5–10% (Track A): below Faire's 15% and GoCoop's 5–15%; against Ankorstore's 0% we compete on service value.
  • Consolidated export as its own track (Track B): solves low-volume, many-SKU demand and protects margin.
  • Published admission criteria (Made Trade, WFTO): enterprise-level certification, product-level spec.
  • Impact card aligned to EcoVadis fields so one input serves every due-diligence request.
  • One standard spec set — MOQ, FOB, lead time, Incoterms, HS code — in the same order on every screen.
  • RFQ answered within 18 hours: the execution bottleneck turned into a service level.
  • Escrow settlement and pre-shipment inspection as the trust layer for Track A.
  • Public channels bring leads; the platform converts them — not a replacement for support programmes.
  • Category sequence: crafts, living, fabric, upcycled goods first; food and cosmetics after licensing and logistics are stable.
